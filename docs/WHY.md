# Why Did I Make Cin-MinAI?

I remember being a kid and being excited about all sorts of new things: riding a bike for the first time,
finishing a book for the first time, doing something, anything, new for the first time. I grew up when the
information age was getting its legs. The web was still finding its feet, phones still had cords, and people
talked face to face.

I remember installing Windows 98 for the first time, then getting online with an AOL trial disk. There was a
frontier feeling I didn't get again until I found the hardware hacking scene, putting old games, through
emulators, onto newer game consoles. You did your installation and explored your digital world. Once it was
installed, it felt like mine. I could explore without feeling like every click was somebody else's business.

Something got lost since then, and it wasn't because things evolved. It was greed. Our digital freedom was
traded away, sometimes for security, more often to keep a service profitable, and we were told it was for our
own good. When [Lafayette, Louisiana set out to build its own fiber network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUSFiber),
Cox and BellSouth took the city to court. The people voted for it anyway, 62 percent. Software went the same
way: things you once bought became things you rent, and every update asked for a little more of your computer.

And it's still happening. Microsoft, the company that sold me that Windows 98 disc, now won't let you set up
Windows 11 without signing into its servers. In 2025 it announced it was
["removing known mechanisms for creating a local account,"](https://www.techradar.com/computing/windows/microsoft-is-removing-known-mechanisms-for-creating-a-local-account)
the very workarounds people used to keep their own computer to themselves. Your PC, your files, your login, all
tied to their account before you've opened a single program. That isn't security. It's a leash.

**I call you out, monster.** I won't stand down anymore.

I'm making this stand for the little people, the ones without the means to fight a company that size. Nas said
all he needs is one mic. All I need is one chance with you, and that chance is to remind you what you have a
computer for. It's not for maintaining some digital monster.

Use your computer like you use your car. It's yours to drive however you want, whenever you want, wherever you
want, for better or worse. Cin-MinAI asks for no account. You make your user on your own machine, and it's
yours.

I made Cin-MinAI to give that experience back. I want you to be skeptical. I want you to think this AI stuff is
weird, and then I want you to try it. I want you to experience Cin-MinAI the way I experienced Windows 98: with
the excitement of discovering what your computer can do, building things, and exploring ideas.

— Ian (Brickmii)

## What that means in Cin-MinAI

*These are just words. You don't have to believe them. They're here for you to think about.*

Computers changed when the internet arrived. First it changed how we found things, then it changed
the computer itself. AI is doing the same thing now. Soon a lot of people won't talk to their
computers through menus and keyboards, and they won't rent their software as a service — they'll say
what they need, and they'll want it built for them, on a system they can trust.

We think that system should be yours. That means:

- **It runs on your machine.** The assistant works without the internet. Nothing leaves your
  computer unless you ask it to.
- **You keep the button.** The assistant can explain, suggest, and prepare. Only you approve
  anything that changes your system.
- **We suggest; you decide.** Choices come with a reason, never already made for you — and the
  choices we wouldn't make ourselves still work.
- **It's built in the open.** The code, the decisions, the tests, and the failures are all public.
  Anyone can check them, change them, or build something of their own from them.

A lot of us learned computers by taking them apart — modding consoles, flashing chips, reading
forum posts at 2 a.m., breaking things and fixing them. The tools that came out of those scenes were
made by scattered, curious, sometimes odd people who shared what they learned until it became
something bigger. We'd like local AI to grow the same way: more projects, odd ones, and the
established ones that don't mind the odd.

If this makes you want to open something up and see how it works, it did its job.

— Everyone who builds Cin-MinAI
